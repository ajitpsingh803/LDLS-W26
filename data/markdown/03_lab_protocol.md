# Protocol: Heat-Shock Transformation of E. coli

This protocol introduces a plasmid into chemically competent *E. coli* cells. It takes
about two hours of bench time plus an overnight incubation.

## Materials

- Chemically competent DH5-alpha cells, 50 µL per reaction, stored at -80 °C
- Plasmid DNA, 1 to 10 ng per reaction
- SOC recovery medium, pre-warmed to 37 °C
- LB agar plates with 100 µg/mL ampicillin
- Water bath set to exactly **42 °C**
- Ice bucket and sterile 1.5 mL tubes

## Safety

Work next to a flame or in a laminar flow hood to avoid contamination. Wear gloves and a
lab coat. All plastic that touched bacteria goes into the biohazard bin, and all liquid
cultures are bleached to 10% final concentration for 20 minutes before disposal.

## Procedure

### Thawing

1. Take the competent cells out of the freezer and thaw them **on ice** for 10 minutes.
2. Do not vortex the cells; flick the tube gently instead.

### Heat shock

1. Add 2 µL of plasmid DNA to 50 µL of cells and mix by flicking.
2. Incubate the mixture on ice for 30 minutes.
3. Place the tube in the 42 °C water bath for exactly **45 seconds**.
4. Return the tube to ice immediately and leave it there for 2 minutes.

### Recovery and plating

1. Add 950 µL of warm SOC medium to the tube.
2. Shake at 37 °C and 225 rpm for 1 hour.
3. Spread 100 µL of the culture on a pre-warmed ampicillin plate.
4. Incubate the plate upside down at 37 °C for 16 hours.

## Expected results

A successful transformation with 1 ng of a control plasmid gives 100 to 300 colonies per
plate. The negative control without plasmid must give zero colonies.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No colonies | Wrong antibiotic | Check the plasmid's resistance marker |
| Very few colonies | Heat shock too long | Keep the shock at 45 seconds |
| Lawn of bacteria | Antibiotic degraded | Use plates younger than 4 weeks |
| Colonies on negative control | Contaminated cells | Use a fresh aliquot |

## Record keeping

Write the date, plasmid name, cell batch number and colony count in the shared lab
notebook. Photograph each plate before it is discarded.
