# Visitor Guide to Port Aldern

Port Aldern is a small harbour city on a northern coast, known for its fish market, its
lighthouse and its steep painted streets. This guide covers the essentials for a first
visit of two or three days.

## Getting there

### By train

Direct trains leave the capital every two hours. The journey takes **3 hours 40
minutes**, and the station is a ten-minute walk from the old harbour.

### By ferry

From May to September a ferry connects Port Aldern with the islands to the west. The
crossing takes 90 minutes and bicycles travel free.

## Neighbourhoods

### Old Harbour

The oldest part of the city, with wooden warehouses turned into cafés. The fish market
opens at 5:30 every morning except Monday.

### Hillside

A maze of staircases and painted houses above the harbour. The **Aldern Lighthouse**
stands at the top of the hill; it was built in 1874 and can be climbed in summer.

### Market District

Modern shops, the central library and most of the hotels. Parking is easiest here.

## Food and drink

Try the smoked herring sandwich at the harbour kiosks and the cloudberry cake sold in
every bakery. Most restaurants close their kitchens at 21:30. Tipping is not expected,
but rounding up the bill is common.

## Practical information

- Currency: euro; cards are accepted almost everywhere.
- Emergency number: **112**.
- The tourist office is next to the train station and opens at 9:00.
- Tap water is safe to drink.

## Day trips

A coastal hiking trail of 14 kilometres leads south to the fishing village of Skarvik.
Buses return to Port Aldern every hour until 19:00, so the hike can be done one way.
